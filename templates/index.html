Preprocessing pipeline
8-step data processing workflow
1
Pull hourly vitals (first 72 ICU h) and labs from derived tables
2
Set implausible values to missing; remove duplicates
3
Adults only; drop stays with onset < 6 h after ICU admission
4
Hourly grid per stay; labs count as known 1 h after the draw
5
Forward fill only (never backward, so no future leakage)
6
Encode gender: M → 1, F → 0
7
Label = Sepsis-3 onset within next 6 h; rows after onset dropped
8
Save 2,827,647 rows × 115 features
Engineered features
Clinical indicators derived from raw vitals
Shock Index
HR / (Systolic BP + 1)

Pulse Pressure
Systolic BP − Diastolic BP
Abnormal pulse pressure indicates hemodynamic instability.

How to read these results

XGBoost is the best of the models tested, flagging the top 5% of hours by risk and catching about 24% of the hours before onset, with 2.8% precision (about 4.7× the 0.6% base rate). Most alerts are still false alarms, and accuracy is not informative here because always predicting “no sepsis” scores 99.4%. The demo on the Predict tab uses a simpler model with only a single snapshot of vitals, age and sex (AUROC about 0.66; at its High level, recall 15% and precision 1.9%). The LSTM and GRU read the last 24 hours of raw measurements; they overfit within a few epochs and scored below XGBoost (AUROC 0.705 and 0.715 vs 0.744). Averaging XGBoost with the GRU gave AUROC 0.747, a difference within the fold-to-fold noise, with lower AUPRC, so XGBoost remains the chosen model. The Predict tab uses the single-snapshot XGBoost model, not the LSTM or GRU. Results are internal validation on one hospital system.
